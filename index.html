Featured project

Condition-dependent antifungal activity in marine sediment-derived bacteria

MSc Applied Biotechnology · University of Westminster · 2023

The project began by screening more than 35 marine sediment isolates against Candida albicans and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. I then studied two isolates in depth, Bacillus altitudinis and Bacillus hwajinpoensis1, against C. albicans NCPF 3179. The question was whether adding chitin to the culture medium changes extracellular antifungal activity, and whether chitinolytic activity or predicted secondary-metabolite pathways could explain it.

The work also found evidence consistent with chitinolytic activity, and predicted biosynthetic potential in both genomes. Clinical diagnostics and experimental science

I work as an Advanced Biomedical Support Worker in Clinical Blood Sciences, in a UKAS ISO 15189-accredited NHS laboratory. A diagnostic result has to be analytically reliable, traceable and reported in the right clinical context.

That shapes how I approach research. An interesting observation is a beginning. A conclusion needs controls, repeats and careful interpretation. My longer-term aim is translational microbiology that links environmental discovery to clinically relevant antimicrobial problems.
