Some microbial chemistry only appears when the environment changes

A nutrient runs short, a competitor appears, a new substrate arrives. Microorganisms respond by changing their metabolism and switching on quiet pathways, and molecules appear that standard culture would never show.

I hold an MSc in Applied Biotechnology (Distinction) from the University of Westminster and work in NHS Clinical Blood Sciences. My MSc research tested whether chitin changes the antifungal activity of marine sediment-derived bacteria against Candida albicans. Chitin-supplemented cultures inhibited C. albicans; unsupplemented ones did not, under the conditions tested. That result left me with the question that drives my interests:

How much antimicrobial potential stays hidden because we have not found the conditions that reveal it?
